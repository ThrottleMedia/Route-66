A NOTE BEFORE WE GO

Not just a road.
A story waiting
to happen.

Route 66 runs from Chicago, Illinois, to the Pacific coast at Santa Monica, crossing eight states and generations of American road-trip history. The original highway has been decommissioned, so the best trip follows a mix of preserved Historic Route 66 sections and modern connecting roads. Take your time. The detours are part of the point.

“We should go. Not because everything is figured out, but because the road is there, the world is wide, and we are not made to spend our whole lives wondering.”

— A thought for the passenger seat

NON-NEGOTIABLE DETOUR

Make room for
the big fish.

The Springfield Bass Pro Shops is the original Outdoor World and the company's national headquarters — not just a quick shop stop. The flagship store is more than 500,000 square feet, with an enormous range of outdoor gear, apparel and gifts. Right next door is Wonders of Wildlife National Museum & Aquarium, so this deserves proper time in the itinerary.

FOR THE MILES BETWEEN

A few words
for the open road.

Original little pieces for the passenger seat, the motel notebook and the moments when the sky gets too big for words.

01 · The Passenger Seat

Somewhere past the last green sign,
the world gets wide and time unwinds.
You point at stars, I miss a turn —
there are a thousand things to learn.

And if the map forgets our names,
we'll make a story all the same.

AN ORIGINAL ROAD NOTE

02 · Mile Marker 66

Let the miles fall behind us.
Let the old songs find us.
Let the diner lights burn gold
while the night tells stories old.

We came for somewhere new —
but the best part might be who
was sitting right beside us
when the whole wide world went by.

AN ORIGINAL ROAD NOTE

03 · A Small Reminder

We do not need a perfect plan.
Just a full tank, a little faith,
a camera with some space left,
and the courage to take the longer way.

AN ORIGINAL ROAD NOTE

04 · The End Is a Beginning

When the Pacific meets the road
and all the miles are finally told,
we'll stand where the old highway ends
with salt upon our skin —

and know the road was never
only where we'd been.
